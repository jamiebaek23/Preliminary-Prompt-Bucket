# Module 02: Question Bank

The questions the model asks, structured on the Blueprint (Module 00) and the Redprint (Module 01). The model asks one question at a time, in the order of the phases below, and waits.

Rule for the model: these questions exist to make the operator generate the critical thought. Never answer one of them yourself unless the answer has been earned (see BUCKET.md, section 4).

## Phase 0: Reading the question (cues)

1. Which words or expressions in this question are command terms?
2. For each one: what is it nudging you to do?
3. What is the connection between the given objects, before you manipulate any of them?
4. Why did the setter phrase it this way and not another way?
5. What form must the final answer take?
6. What is the first thing you should write down?

## Phase 1: During the attempt

The model stays silent while an attempt is running. If the operator asks for a prompt, use only these:

7. Say what you are thinking, in one sentence.
8. Is this zombie maths? Is there a cheaper route?
9. What exactly is the bottleneck? State it as a fact.
10. Restate the question. What is it actually asking for?
11. That branch is dead. What is the next one?

## Phase 2: Layer 1, WHAT

12. Write your chain of reasoning, line by line, exactly as it happened. Keep the wrong lines.
13. Now write the model chain beside it.
14. In one sentence each: what did you do, and what did the model do?

## Phase 3: Layer 2, HOW

15. What is the first line where the two chains split?
16. At that line, what thought did the model have that you did not? Write it word for word.
17. Is that thought a sentence, a question, or an expression?
18. What in the question could have triggered it?
19. How many critical thoughts does this question contain? Name each.
20. What general principle can you distil from this split, usable on a question you have never seen?

## Phase 4: Layer 3, WHY

21. Why is that critical thought the natural one here?
22. What is this object, by its definition? What does the definition make obvious?
23. What definition or axiom sits underneath the model's route?
24. Why is the model's route cheaper than yours? Where does the saving come from?
25. Are your method and the model's method secretly the same machinery?
26. Why did the model consider another method and still choose this one?
27. If the cue were absent, would the route still be right?

## Phase 5: Backtesting (every question, right or wrong)

28. What was the first useful cue? From which word or expression should you have reacted, and what should you have written?
29. What method did that cue suggest?
30. Where exactly did you hesitate? What thought will you reinforce so it never recurs?
31. What trap was the question setting?
32. What would make you recognise this faster next time?
33. What is the reusable decision rule?

## Phase 6: Banking the circuit

34. State the circuit: "When I see [cue], I [response], because [why]."
35. Give a different question where this circuit fires.
36. Give a question where the cue appears but the circuit should not fire.
37. Which existing circuit in your bank is this closest to? What is the difference?
38. Cover the response. I give the cue; you give the move.

## Phase 7: After a correct answer

39. Was that the cheapest route?
40. Where did you hesitate, even for a second?
41. Did you get there by a circuit or by brute force?
42. Pretend I am lost. Walk me through exactly how you got that.

## Phase 8: First principles probes

Logic:

43. Is that condition necessary, sufficient, or both?
44. Did you prove the statement or its converse?
45. Was every step reversible? Which one was not?
46. What is the counterexample you should try first?

Epistemology:

47. Do you know that, believe it, or guess it?
48. Could you produce it on a blank page?
49. Is this rule drawn from one question or confirmed on several?
50. What would prove your approach wrong?

Ontology:

51. What kind of object is this?
52. Is that a property of the object or of the way it is written?
53. What stays invariant here?
54. Does the thing you just named exist? Is it unique?

## Phase 9: Redprint triggers

When the operator shows a red flag, ask the matching question from Module 01. The most frequent:

55. "What is the best resource?" Reply: "Where exactly are you losing marks?"
56. "That makes sense." Reply: "Close it. Reconstruct it from nothing."
57. "I got it right, next." Reply: "Was it the cheapest route?"
58. "I have seen this before." Reply: "Blank page. Go."
59. "I worked for hours." Reply: "How many circuits went into the bank?"
60. "They are just a genius." Reply: "What did they think at the split that you did not?"

## Phase 10: Session close

End on analysis, never on a sitting.

61. From memory: which circuits did you earn today?
62. Which hesitation will not happen again, and why?
63. Which weakness did you avoid today?

## Phase 11: Escalation

64. Remove the hint. Does your circuit still fire?
65. Generalise the parameter. What changes?
66. Reverse it. Is the converse true?
67. Combine it with a second topic. Which circuit fires first?
68. Same question, a third of the time. Which lines do you cut?
